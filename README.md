## CAT 2026 Syllabus Overview

The CAT exam consists of three sections:

- VARC (Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension)  
- DILR (Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning)  
- QA (Quantitative Aptitude)  

Each section is time-bound, making sectional preparation and time management extremely important.



## Section-Wise Syllabus & Preparation Strategy

### VARC

**Topics Covered:**
- Reading Comprehension  
- Para Jumbles  
- Para Summary  
- Odd One Out  

**Preparation Strategy:**
- Read newspapers and editorials daily  
- Solve 2–3 RC passages regularly  
- Practice verbal ability questions weekly  
- Focus on accuracy before speed  



### DILR

**Topics Covered:**
- Bar Graphs & Pie Charts  
- Caselets  
- Seating Arrangement  
- Logical Puzzles  
- Venn Diagrams  

**Preparation Strategy:**
- Practice diverse puzzle patterns  
- Solve 3–4 sets daily  
- Analyze solved sets to understand logic  
- Improve set selection during mocks  


### QA

**Topics Covered:**
- Arithmetic (Percentages, Profit & Loss, Time & Work)  
- Algebra  
- Geometry  
- Number System  
- Modern Math  

**Preparation Strategy:**
- Start with strong Arithmetic fundamentals  
- Maintain a formula notebook  
- Practice topic-wise questions  
- Gradually move to mixed timed practice

## Daily Study Routine for CAT 2026

### For College Students:
- 4–5 hours daily  
- Focus on 1 section per day  
- 1 RC practice daily  
- 2–3 DILR sets  
- 15–20 QA questions  

**Weekly Plan:**
- 1 sectional test per subject  
- 1 full-length mock  



### For Working Professionals:
- 2–3 hours on weekdays  
- 4–5 hours on weekends  
- 1 mock every weekend  
- Dedicated weekly revision session  

Consistency matters more than long study hours.



## Mock Test Strategy

Mocks are the backbone of CAT 2026 preparation.

- Start mocks after completing basic concepts  
- Attempt 2–3 mocks weekly in the final 3 months  
- Spend 2–3 hours analyzing each mock  
- Identify weak areas and refine strategy
